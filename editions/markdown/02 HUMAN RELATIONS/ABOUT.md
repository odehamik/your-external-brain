# Who belongs in the record?

Someone may matter to the work without needing a file about them.

This room is optional. Keep only the work-relevant relations the person chooses and is entitled to record. A role or organization may be enough. Names, contact details, private history, and cultural affiliations are not compulsory fields waiting to be filled.

Ask what the record is for, where it belongs, who may read it, and what remains unrecorded. A relation that belongs to one project can stay there. A general map may point toward it only when that pointer is permitted and reveals no more than it should.

Keep the person’s account, documentary evidence, and the machine’s interpretation distinguishable. A sentence in an email does not establish someone’s intentions for all time. Do not infer alliances, conflicts, authority, Indigenous identity, or psychological traits.

A mail helper may eventually propose updates from messages explicitly opened to it. That does not provide general inbox access or sending permission. Changes in responsibilities need evidence. A draft promise is still a draft.

We can remember what matters here without making everyone who passed through the work permanently searchable.
