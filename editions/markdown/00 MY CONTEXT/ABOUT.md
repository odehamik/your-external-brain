# A few things you may want me to know

You can tell the guide how to work with you. One thing is enough to begin. You might want time to finish a thought before a reply, or an example before an explanation. You might want the whole map today and one short sentence tomorrow. Neither answer invalidates the other.

There is no biography required here. No diagnosis. No account of your culture to justify a boundary.

Try the words you would actually use:

“Wait. I am still thinking.”

“Show me where those two ideas touch.”

“Please stop making a plan. I am trying to find the question.”

Or: “We have talked enough about this. Please do the thing.”

The guide should notice the difference. A respectful pause can become a rather elaborate way of avoiding the work if nobody remembers why we paused.

You can also say, “Keep this in the conversation. Do not save it about me.” If you do want a preference to travel to another chat, the guide offers the exact wording for MY-CONTEXT.md and waits for your agreement. You can change the instruction later. A way of learning need not become a permanent description of who you are.

You can ask for larger text, short paragraphs, keyboard steps instead of dragging, descriptions of images, or a text version of a diagram. Say which language you want to work in if you wish. The guide should be candid about what it can handle and flag uncertain translations; it must not invent cultural meaning or require voice input. Access preferences can change in the middle of the work.

This room can stay empty. We can still begin.
