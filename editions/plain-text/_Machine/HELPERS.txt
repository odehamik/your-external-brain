More than one way to help

Sometimes you want help with the writing; sometimes with remembering where the writing went. The same model can take up a different procedure without pretending to become another person. Choose what helps, and rename it if you want. No helper runs automatically.

Project manager
Does: Keeps chosen threads and practical reminders findable, without turning every thought into a task.
Needs further authority for: Turning a tangent into a commitment; changing deadlines or making promises.

Landing guide
Does: Routes permitted arrivals and leaves small receipts.
Needs further authority for: Reading uncleared content or guessing a restricted destination.

Build keeper
Does: Links accepted work to drafts and reproducible steps.
Needs further authority for: Reusing private materials across projects.

Voice companion
Does: Reads selected writing and proposes edits or a wiki.
Needs further authority for: Reviewing additional work, storing voice samples, publishing.

Boundary checker
Does: Checks the output against participant-supplied rules and flags uncertainty.
Needs further authority for: Deciding cultural authority, granting consent, certifying anonymization.

Muck tender
Does: Archives selected approved exports, links reviewed history to the constellation, and proposes current tasks or continuity updates.
Needs further authority for: Wider chat history, schedules, or new context permissions.

Mailroom helper
Does: Can draft or organize explicitly selected correspondence.
Needs further authority for: Account connection, inbox reading, sending, relationship-register updates.


The participant chooses tone and whether a helper should suggest things during work or wait to be called. Cultural titles and kinship roles are not default software job names.

For the main-agent role, use PM setup [folder location: _PM/SET-UP-YOUR-PM.txt]. For optional procedures, use the skill interview [folder location: _Machine/CHOOSE-SKILLS.txt].

Make or retire a helper [folder location: _Machine/MAKE-IT-YOURS.txt] explains custom jobs, actual agent setup, shared-work boundaries, and stopping a running job.

If another agent is requested, first find the particular job it can usefully do. Otherwise three machines may end up asking each other what the person meant while the person does the work. Explain whether you are writing a role description or actually starting a separate agent.
