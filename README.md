# Your External Brain

You have acquired a second brain. Please do not give it jurisdiction over the first.

A small, customizable folder for keeping work where you can find it and deciding how an AI participates. Built for an Indigenous AI gathering through Maya Chacaby’s work. Participants bring their own relations and governing protocols.

## Get the tools ready — one route is enough

Already have an AI you use and a way to open your files? Skip the parts you have done. You do not need to install the entire table. That is how a workshop becomes an unplanned career in technical support.

### 1. Choose the AI arrangement you are comfortable using

You have thought about the relations, harms, costs, and possibilities involved, and chosen what you are willing to use for this work. That might be a corporate service such as Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini, including a subscription if you choose. It might be an open-weight model running on your computer, or a harness that connects a model to tools. A harness is the surrounding software that lets a model work with things such as files.

These choices can be revisited. Paying for a service does not settle its ethics; using an open-weight model does not settle them either. What matters here includes where the work goes, who can access it, what your governing relations permit, and whether you are comfortable operating the software on your device. A free account may be enough for this starter conversation; the kit does not require a purchase.

Install only on a device where you are permitted to do so. Enter passwords in the actual app or website, never in an AI conversation. If an account or download is unavailable, ask your facilitator for another permitted route. Choosing an app does not give it access to all your files.

**A local model or harness, if this is your chosen route:**

An open-weight model makes its model weights available under its licence; it can still be hosted remotely. A harness running on your desktop may also call a remote model. Ask where the selected model and tools actually send data. Never paste an API key into the starter conversation. A chat subscription and API usage can have separate billing; check the service before assuming one pays for the other.

If this route is not already familiar, ask for help before installing it. You can explore the folder or use an existing permitted chat while deciding. There is no bonus round for installing everything.

### 2. Download and unpack ONE edition of this folder

Choose **Plain Text** or **Markdown** using the ZIP links in the repository, or use the ZIP your facilitator sent. If you are already reading this inside an extracted folder, this step is done.

On GitHub, open the ZIP’s page and select **Download raw file** or the download icon. You need the participant ZIP, not the whole repository. No GitHub account is needed for this public repository. Ask the facilitator for the ZIP directly if downloads are blocked on your network.

Find the downloaded ZIP, usually in Downloads. On a Mac, double-click it to extract the folder. On Windows, right-click it and choose **Extract All**, then follow the prompts. Use your device’s archive/file app on other systems, or ask for help. Move the extracted folder somewhere you recognize and are permitted to keep it. Keep the original ZIP separately if you want a fresh copy later.

Open the extracted folder containing README and START-HERE.txt. Work in that folder, not inside the ZIP preview. A ZIP is useful for delivery; living inside one makes saving unnecessarily mysterious.

### 3A. Markdown route — open the folder in Obsidian

1. [Download Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/download) for your device, install it, and open it.
2. In its vault chooser, choose **Open folder as vault**, then **Open**. Select the extracted **Your External Brain - Markdown** folder containing README.md. A vault is simply the folder Obsidian is opening; you do not need to create the kit again. If another vault is open, use the vault switcher to manage/open vaults. [Official vault-opening instructions](https://obsidian.md/help/manage-vaults).
3. Open **Settings** using the gear icon. Under **Files and links**, turn on **Show all file types**. This shows other formats; it does not give Obsidian a native editor for every one.
4. Suggested settings: **Automatically update internal links** on; **Use Wikilinks** off for portable Markdown links. Under **Editor**, choose **Live preview** if you prefer formatted notes. Under **Appearance**, adjust font size to suit you. [Official settings guide](https://obsidian.md/help/settings).
5. Open README.md. For START-HERE.txt, use your computer’s text editor if Obsidian cannot open it internally. Right-click the file and use an available **Open in default app** option, or open it through Finder/File Explorer. No community plugin is required for this.

Those are suggested starting settings, not a copy of Maya’s personal configuration. You can change them. Obsidian displays your files; opening a vault does not automatically connect an LLM to it. Sync services and AI plugins are separate choices and are not needed to begin.

### 3B. Plain Text route — skip Obsidian

Use an app that opens **and edits** .txt files. You may already have one:

- **Windows:** open **Notepad** from Start. [Official Notepad download if needed](https://apps.microsoft.com/detail/9msmlrh6lzf3).
- **Mac:** open **TextEdit** from Applications. [Apple’s TextEdit guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/textedit/welcome/mac). Keep notes as plain text and save with .txt rather than rich-text .rtf.
- **Linux:** use your existing text editor; [GNOME Text Editor](https://apps.gnome.org/TextEditor/) is one option.
- **Phone/tablet:** use a trusted text editor that can open and save files in your chosen folder; ask your facilitator for device-specific help.

Open **00 OPEN ME FIRST.txt**, then **START-HERE.txt**. If double-clicking does not work, open the editor first and use its **File → Open** command. You do not need Word or a Markdown app. Folder locations written in the text may not be clickable; they tell you where to look.

### 4. Let the guide begin

In your text editor, open **START-HERE.txt** and copy all the words: **Command+A, Command+C** on Mac, or **Ctrl+A, Ctrl+C** on Windows/Linux. Select text inside the open document, not the whole folder. On a touch device, use its text-selection and Copy controls.

Open a new conversation in your chosen AI app. Paste into its message box (**Command+V** or **Ctrl+V**) and send. The guide should welcome you, ask one relevant setup question, and wait. Answer in your own words. If the app cannot accept the whole starter text, ask your facilitator to help attach just that starter file or choose a suitable input route; do not upload personal material to solve a text-length problem.

If it summarizes the prompt, say: **“Begin guiding me now. Give me the first step and wait.”**

Before bringing your own work, ask the guide to help check [file permissions and training/data settings](editions/markdown/%5FMachine/SETTINGS-AND-STUCK.md). It can begin with an empty note while settings are unresolved. Downloading the app is not the same as agreeing that it may read your life.

Official links and the named setup controls were checked on 10 September 2026. If the screen differs, tell the guide what it says; it should check current official help rather than insist the button must be there.

## Inside the folder

A landing for permitted files, context you choose to keep, projects and publications, relation and boundary instructions, an optional PM, reusable skills, and a Muck archive for selected old conversations. The guide introduces these when useful; they are not a reading assignment.

The handshake locates the machine’s material and political relations and gives responsibilities consequences in the next action. Participants can change its wording. The kit does not prescribe one Indigenous ceremony, grant cultural authority to an AI, or make privacy guarantees through folder names.
