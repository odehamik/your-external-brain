SET UP YOUR APP

From installed app
to an open folder.

This is the part between step 1 and step 3 that nobody films for the tutorial. Seven short steps. Do them in order, and have someone sit beside you for step 5 if you can.

Menus checked against each company’s help pages on 20 November 2026. Companies move their menus. If your screen disagrees with these words, believe the screen and ask your facilitator.

Which app are you using?

Only your app’s steps will show. You can switch any time.

  1. Back up your computer first.

    You are about to let a company’s program read and write files on your machine. It will ask before it acts, and it will only see the folder you give it. Back up anyway. You settle the house before you invite something into it, and a backup turns the worst case into an inconvenience.

    Mac

    1. Plug in an external drive.
    2. Open System Settings → General → Time Machine.
    3. Choose Add Backup Disk, pick the drive, and let the first backup finish.

    Windows

    1. Plug in a USB stick or external drive.
    2. Copy the folders you care about onto it: Documents, Pictures, Desktop, and anything else you would grieve.

    The Windows Backup app sends your files to OneDrive, which is the thing step 4 will help you avoid. A plain copy onto a drive you can hold is enough for today.

    No drive? At the very least, keep a copy of the folder you are about to connect. The kit itself cannot break: if anything goes wrong with it, download it again.

  2. Install the desktop app.

    Use the company’s own download page. The website version in your browser looks almost identical and cannot open folders on your computer, so it will not work for this.

    Download Claude for desktop

    1. Download the version for your computer (Mac or Windows) and install it.
    2. Open it and sign in. Type your password only into the app’s own sign-in screen.

    The easiest folder connection in Claude, called Cowork, needs a paid plan. On a free plan there is another route in step 5; it works, with one extra click.

    Download ChatGPT for desktop

    1. Download the version for your computer (Mac or Windows) and install it.
    2. Open it and sign in. Type your password only into the app’s own sign-in screen.

    Folder work happens in the desktop app’s Work mode. What is included depends on your plan; if Work or local folders are locked, tell your facilitator.

    On a work or school computer, ask before installing anything. Your administrator may control what is allowed.

  3. Close the training door.

    Both companies can use your conversations to train future models unless you switch it off. One switch each. Do it now, before anything of yours goes in.

    1. Click your name or initials (bottom left).
    2. Choose Settings, then Privacy.
    3. Turn off Help improve Claude.
    1. Click your profile picture or name.
    2. Choose Settings, then Data controls.
    3. Turn off Improve the model for everyone.

    What this switch does: your new chats stop being used to train the company’s models. What it leaves alone: the conversation still travels to the company’s computers to be answered, and they still keep it for a while. The switch is a boundary. It does not make anything local. Material that must never leave your hands belongs on a local model, or nowhere near a machine.

  4. Make your folder somewhere the cloud cannot reach.

    This one catches almost everyone, and it is invisible until it bites. Windows quietly moves your Desktop and Documents into OneDrive. Macs can do the same with iCloud. Most people never agreed to it, and most never notice.

    Why it matters here:

    • The AI writes a file while the cloud is mid-sync, and you get two copies with a computer’s name stuck on the end of one.
    • Some files are only “in the cloud” and not really on your computer, so the app cannot read them.
    • On a university or work account, that OneDrive belongs to the institution and sits under its data rules. For community knowledge, that is a question of who holds what.

    Windows: check, then fix

    Check. Open File Explorer and go to the folder where you put the kit. Click the address bar at the top. If the path contains OneDrive, or the files show little cloud icons, you are inside it.

    Fix.

    1. In File Explorer, click the address bar, type %USERPROFILE% and press Enter. This is your own user folder, and OneDrive does not manage it.
    2. Right-click an empty space and choose New → Folder. Name it External Brain.
    3. Move the kit’s files into it.

    You end up with something like C:\Users\yourname\External Brain. On a managed computer, leave OneDrive’s own settings alone; making the folder here is enough.

    Mac: check, then fix

    Check. Open System Settings, click your name at the top, then iCloud → iCloud Drive. If Desktop & Documents Folders is on, those two folders live in iCloud.

    Fix.

    1. In Finder, choose Go → Home from the menu bar. This is your own home folder (the one with a house icon).
    2. Choose File → New Folder and name it External Brain.
    3. Move the kit’s files into it.

    Google Drive, Dropbox and Box folders have the same problem. Keep the brain outside all of them.

  5. Give the app that one folder.

    Paid plan: Cowork

    1. Open the Claude app and switch to Cowork.
    2. Add a folder (or create a project from a folder) and choose your External Brain folder.
    3. If your computer asks whether Claude may access files in that location, allow it.

    Claude can only work in the folders you choose, and it asks before deleting anything. Keep the app open while you work: when it is closed, it cannot reach the folder.

    Free plan: the Filesystem extension

    1. Open Settings, then Extensions.
    2. Find Filesystem and install it.
    3. When it asks which folders to allow, choose only your External Brain folder.
    4. Quit Claude completely and open it again.
    1. Open the ChatGPT desktop app and choose Work from the mode switcher.
    2. Open a local folder (from the plus button, or by adding a local project) and choose your External Brain folder.
    3. If your computer asks whether ChatGPT may access files in that location, allow it.

    Work threads that use local files stay on that computer. They will not show up on your phone or the website, and that is expected.

    Choose the External Brain folder itself. Never your whole drive, your home folder, or Documents. The smallest door that does the job.

  6. Try something harmless.

    Before the real work, check the door opens. Start a new chat and send these one at a time:

    1. “List the files in my External Brain folder.” It should name START-HERE and the other kit files.
    2. “Make a file called hello.md that says hello.” When it asks permission, allow it.
    3. Now open the folder yourself and look. The file is there.

    That third step matters most. You saw it with your own eyes, on your own machine. From here the machine stops being a rumour.

    Then go back to step 3 on the main page and paste the opening.

  7. What you just allowed, and what you didn’t.

    Connecting a folder lets the app read and write files inside that one folder. Nothing else on your computer.

    Both apps also offer something larger: control of your screen, mouse and other programs. This workshop does not need it. If an app asks for screen recording, accessibility, or “control your computer” permissions, you can say no today and everything here still works. If you do turn it on later, turn it on for a reason, watch what it does, and turn it off when the job is done.

    When a permission box appears, read it. Allow once is a good habit until you trust what it does. You can remove a folder or an extension at any time from the same place you added it. Grant the smallest thing that does the job: that is a protocol, and the machine can learn it.

Stuck?

The app says it can’t see my folder.

Check that you are in the desktop app, not the website. Check that you added the folder (step 5), then quit the app completely and reopen it. Ask: “Which folders can you actually access right now?”

The files are there but it can’t read some of them.

You are probably inside OneDrive or iCloud, and those files live in the cloud. Go back to step 4 and make the folder in your own user folder.

I see two copies of a file with a strange name on the end.

That is a sync conflict: the cloud and the AI wrote at the same time. Same fix, step 4. Keep whichever copy has what you want, and move the other into a folder called “old”.

Cowork, Work, or Extensions is missing or locked.

Update the app to the latest version. Some of these need a paid plan, and on a work or school account your administrator may have turned them off. Tell your facilitator; there is always the slower route of pasting text in and saving replies yourself.

A permission box is asking for more than one folder.

Say no, and try again choosing only the External Brain folder. If it insists on more, stop and ask your facilitator.
