An external brain is a folder of notes, work, and instructions you can see, change, move, and keep. You decide where things live and which parts a machine may use. A new chat can begin with the context you select, instead of relying on what a company’s memory feature happens to retain.

This is a small practice kit. Try the arrangement, change its names, add rooms, refuse bits that do not fit. You are learning how the pieces work together, not signing a lifelong treaty with a folder.

Who is here with this work?

You bring relations and inherited responsibilities, including ones you do not need to explain to a machine. AI arrives through land, water, extracted materials, energy, human labour, and corporate systems. We cannot make those histories disappear by giving the software a pleasant greeting.

Here, acknowledgement has consequences: what may be read, what must stay out, whose permission matters, and how work returns to people. This method comes through Maya Chacaby’s Anishinaabe work. Participants bring their own governing relations; the kit does not prescribe a shared Indigenous ceremony or give AI cultural authority.

Plain Text: which app opens it?

Windows: Notepad. Mac: TextEdit; keep files as plain text (.txt), not rich text (.rtf). Linux: your usual text editor. On a phone or tablet, use a trusted editor that can save back to your chosen folder. Open the editor first and use File → Open if double-clicking fails.

Markdown: open it in Obsidian
  1. Install and open Obsidian.
  2. Choose Open folder as vault and select the extracted Markdown folder. A vault is simply the folder you are opening.
  3. In Settings → Files and links, enable Show all file types. This displays files; it does not add editors for every format.
  4. Suggested: Automatically update internal links on; Use Wikilinks off for portable links. Choose Editor → Live preview and adjust Appearance → font size to suit you.
  5. Open README.md. Open START-HERE.txt in your normal text editor if Obsidian cannot display it.

No plugins or sync service needed. Obsidian does not connect a model to your files automatically. Official vault help.

The guide should acknowledge its part, ask one useful setup question, and wait for you. After your first saved note, it offers a walk through your context, boundaries, PM, projects, and the other rooms—one at a time, with permission to skip or pause. It should notice useful things to keep and ask about the exact words and destination; you do not need to remember to say “put this in my brain.” Nothing you say is automatically a permanent note. You can be casual, formal, uncertain, funny, or quiet. You do not owe the machine a job-interview voice.

It summarized the instructions instead of starting.

Tell it: “Begin guiding me now. Give me the first step and wait.” If that still fails, tell your facilitator what happened. Models do not all follow instructions equally well.

It cannot see or save my files.

The machine may have described a door it does not possess. This happens rather a lot for something so confident about architecture.

Say: “Check what tools you actually have. If you cannot open this folder, help me paste only the needed text and save your reply myself.” Naming a folder in chat does not connect it. Grant access only to material you intend it to use.

A permission, setting, or download is blocking me.

Tell the guide your device, the app, and the exact message on screen, leaving out passwords and private details. Ask for one next step. If the account or device is managed, ask its administrator or your facilitator. You can pause and explore the empty starter folder.

The pieces between you and a response. Knowing which piece does what gives you more choices.

03 / GOOD QUESTIONS

Before the machine
gets carried away.

Does this give me more control?

It gives you an inspectable place for context, instructions, and decisions. You can choose what to include, correct, withhold, export, or delete from your own files. You still need to check what the app actually reads and what the model does. Instructions are not enforcement, and deleting a local note does not delete copies already sent to a service.

Will it save tokens or money?

It can reduce repeated input when you provide a small relevant note instead of a whole archive. That is a possible workflow benefit, not a measured promise for this kit. Files still use tokens when the model reads them; long instructions, tool calls, and repeated reads can increase use.

Tokens are the units a model processes. Cost also depends on the provider, model, subscription or API billing, and caching. Caching can change the price of repeated input without removing that input. Do not assume a shorter conversation lowers a flat subscription price. Provider example: OpenAI’s API caching documentation.

Does it replace the model’s memory?

It lets your own approved notes carry continuity across chats and providers, reducing dependence on a provider’s memory feature. The new chat must actually receive the relevant notes. This kit does not automatically disable service memory or change account settings. Different models may respond differently to the same files.

Where do cultural protocols enter?

In the relation instructions and in each decision about access, use, interpretation, and sharing. The relevant people determine authority. Individual consent does not automatically permit use of collectively governed knowledge. CARE may help frame responsibilities; OCAP is specifically First Nations, not a universal label for every Indigenous people. A prompt cannot certify compliance.

You can keep responsibilities unnamed and still set a boundary. Nothing here asks you to upload ceremony, sacred knowledge, or community records. CARE principles · First Nations OCAP.

Is this website an AI? Does it collect my work?

This is a static guide. There is no chat connection, upload form, analytics script, or account on this page. Copying the opening puts text on your clipboard; it does not send it to a model. You decide where to paste it. Hosting providers still handle normal web requests and may keep access logs. External links take you to other services with their own policies.

Can I use it offline or without JavaScript?

The downloaded folder works offline in a text editor. A hosted AI normally needs internet; a local model needs to be installed and available first. This guide’s sections and downloads remain usable without JavaScript. The starter file is downloadable if the copy button is unavailable.

Must I be funny or share personal things?

No. You can be formal, quiet, direct, or still finding words. Use “Plainer wording” above for quieter versions of selected passages, and ask your AI for plain language. Neither intimacy nor humour is an admission requirement.

How do I come back next time?

Keep your folder somewhere you recognize and back it up in a way your permissions allow. Open the relevant return-note in the next session and share only what is needed. Ask the model to confirm what it can actually see. Review proposed updates before saving them.
